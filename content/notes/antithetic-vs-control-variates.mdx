# Antithetic vs Control Variates: What Actually Reduced My Standard Error

## The Computational Challenge
Standard crude Monte Carlo simulation converges at rate $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{N})$. To achieve an extra decimal place of precision, you must increase the path budget by a factor of 100. In high-frequency derivatives valuation, variance reduction is essential to compute Greeks in real time.

## 1. Antithetic Variates
Antithetic sampling pairs every standard normal draw $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ with its reflection $-Z$. Because the payoff function $f(Z)$ is monotonic with respect to the driving Brownian motion:
$$\text{Cov}(f(Z), f(-Z)) < 0$$
The variance of the paired average estimator $\frac{f(Z) + f(-Z)}{2}$ is:
$$\text{Var}\left(\frac{f(Z) + f(-Z)}{2}\right) = \frac{\text{Var}(f(Z)) + \text{Cov}(f(Z), f(-Z))}{2} < \frac{\text{Var}(f(Z))}{2}$$
In our tests on European calls, antithetic sampling reduced variance by approximately **1.8x to 2.2x** at virtually zero extra computational cost.

## 2. Control Variates
For path-dependent instruments like arithmetic Asian options (where no closed-form analytic solution exists), we anchor the estimator to a correlated instrument with a known analytical price: the European Call $C_{\text{BS}}$.
The control-variate estimator is:
$$\hat{Y}_{\text{CV}} = \hat{Y} - c^*(\hat{X} - \mathbb{E}[X])$$
Where $c^* = \frac{\text{Cov}(Y, X)}{\text{Var}(X)}$ is estimated from the simulation sample.

Because the arithmetic average price $\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M S_{t_m}$ is strongly correlated with terminal price $S_T$ ($\rho \approx 0.88$), the control variate soaked up the bulk of the diffusion variance, delivering a **3.4x to 4.1x variance reduction ratio**.
